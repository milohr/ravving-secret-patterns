# Solos pack: research notes (2026-10-12, reworked)

The pack's first version (2026-10-11, commit 3d3f71f) was bebop: Parker on alto, Coltrane's
sheets of sound and Countdown reharm, Baker at a cool medium tempo. The user wanted another
mood: **moody, prolonged and passionate, no fast passages**, after the John Coltrane Quartet's
*Ballads*. The pack is now six ballad solos (four saxophone in Coltrane's ballad voice, two
trumpet in Chet Baker's) with their brushes, bass and chords. Nothing is quoted. The album's
songs are standards still under copyright, and so are the recorded solos. The harmonic moods
and the way of playing were studied, and every line, progression and rhythm is new. The pack
is `sources/solos`.

## The album: *Ballads* (Impulse! A-32, 1963)

- **Who and when.** John Coltrane (tenor), McCoy Tyner (piano), Jimmy Garrison (bass; Reggie
  Workman on "It's Easy to Remember"), Elvin Jones (drums). Recorded at Van Gelder's on
  21 December 1961, 18 September and 13 November 1962, and released in January 1963
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballads_(John_Coltrane_album))).
- **The songs** (all standards, all short): Say It (Over and Over Again) 4:18, You Don't Know
  What Love Is 5:15, Too Young to Go Steady 4:23, All or Nothing at All 3:39, I Wish I Knew
  4:54, What's New 3:47, It's Easy to Remember 2:49, Nancy (With the Laughing Face) 3:10.
- **How it was made.** Gene Lees reports that the quartet had not played these tunes before.
  They discussed each one, wrote out the changes, rehearsed for half an hour, and recorded it
  in one take, except All or Nothing at All. The usual story is that Bob Thiele asked for
  ballads to answer the "anti-jazz" criticism. The tale of a damaged mouthpiece is disputed
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballads_(John_Coltrane_album));
  [MusicWeb International](https://musicweb-international.com/jazz/2002/Apr02/Coltrane_ballads.htm)).
- **Coltrane's playing.** The reviews agree on a few points. There are no "sheets of sound":
  he plays the melodies of the standards with "unusual restraint", and the improvising stays
  modest. What carries the music is "tone, space and melodic concentration": a "woody", "reedy"
  sound, a "swooning" tenor, "elegantly restrained" playing with "plenty of passion". The
  breath shapes the phrases, and Tyner's chords let him hold a phrase back or cut it short
  ([Positive Feedback](https://positive-feedback.com/reviews/music-reviews/john-coltrane-quartet-ballads/);
  [SRF](https://www.srf.ch/kultur/musik/musik-balladen-die-zweite-seele-des-john-coltrane):
  "ein grosser Melodiker", "berückend schöner Ton"). The same restraint is found on *John
  Coltrane and Johnny Hartman* (1963): the melodies "as they are", "a very little
  embellishment" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2011/06/17/4555664/john-coltrane-john-coltrane-and-johnny-hartman)).
- **The form of a track.** You Don't Know What Love Is states the melody rubato, then has a
  swinging, very straight tenor solo, then a rubato restatement to close
  ([Michael's jazz blog](https://michaelsjazzblog.com/?p=32)). The song is a 32-bar AABA in
  minor: G minor originally, F minor in most fake books.
- **The band.** Say It shows off Tyner's "elegant chordal accompaniment" as much as the tenor.
  Elvin Jones's "masterful brushwork" stands out on Too Young to Go Steady, and his
  unaccompanied introduction to All or Nothing at All shows "the lyrical possibilities of the
  entire kit". Garrison takes no solo but is "a steady, authoritative presence"
  ([Apple Music editorial notes](https://music.apple.com/album/1440747672)). Jones "shapes the
  pulse through brushes and cymbal detail" rather than display.
- **Tyner's voicings.** He moved from voicings in thirds to fourths. His usual left hand is a
  low root with its fifth or octave, then a jump to stacked fourths, and both hands build
  cluster voicings
  ([Piano with Willie](https://pianowithwillie.com/quartal-fourth-voicings/);
  [jazz.com](https://jazz.com/encyclopedia/tyner-mccoy);
  [jazzguitar.be](https://jazzguitar.be/blog/quartal-chords-harmony-voicings-for-guitar)).
- **Tempos and keys.** No source gives the recordings' BPM or keys, and I could not listen to
  them. The pack uses the brief's 55–75 BPM. The track lengths fit that range: a 32-bar chorus
  at 60 lasts 2:08, so It's Easy to Remember's 2:49 is about a chorus and a third. Eb major
  suits the tenor's ballad register, and its relative minor (C minor) holds the minor moods.

## Baker's ballads

- An "introverted, plaintive tone" and a "relaxed, lyrical style". His reading of a melody is
  "humble" and makes an intimate atmosphere
  ([jazz.com on My Funny Valentine, 1952](https://jazz.com/music/chet-baker-my-funny-valentine-1952)).
  In the Mulligan quartet's instrumental version he plays in the trumpet's lower register, with
  "minimal inflection" and a brooding tone
  ([uDiscover](https://www.udiscovermusic.com/?p=2565104)).
- From the first version's notes: a sparse style with no interest in speed
  ([Hackworth](https://martinhackworth.substack.com/p/chet-bakers-long-goodbye)), and simple
  phrases moving in small steps around the chord's tensions (the USFQ theses on his
  [But Not for Me](https://repositorio.usfq.edu.ec/bitstream/23000/10918/1/128103.pdf) and
  [Autumn Leaves](https://repositorio.usfq.edu.ec/bitstream/23000/10465/1/129947.pdf) solos).
  Michael David Moore's Oklahoma State thesis compares five of his recordings of My Funny
  Valentine ([shareok](https://shareok.org/handle/11244/5856); not read).

## Into the pack

- **One scale:** Eb major, with the minor moods on degree 6 (C minor: the line cliché, the
  ii–V Dø–G7alt) and one pedal vamp on degree 2 (F Dorian). Notes and chords from outside the
  key use ALTER and chromatic voicings: the minor iv Abm6 with its Cb, the backdoor Db9,
  C7b9/alt, E°7, G7's B.
- **Tempo and feel:** 56–70 BPM on the 16-step grid. The templates are 64 (Solos) and 58
  (Solos · Late Night), with the eighths swung a little (56, 54). The rubato comes from the
  nudges: every long note is 8–16 % of a step late, and the ornaments rush in early (<2–6).
- **Long tones:** most notes last 6–12 steps (1.5–3 s at 60), with a beat or half a bar of
  air between phrases.
- **Swells and passion:** a single held note keeps one velocity, so each swell is written
  across the phrase: it starts soft (@60–70), rises to its peak and falls back. The MODS give
  every note a slower attack, so it swells in: ENVELOPE/ATTACK +2400 timecents on the sax,
  +3600 on the trumpet. Devotion's cry comes three times at @104–110. The tenor's peak is the
  high Eb5, F5 only in Devotion.
- **Flurries only as ornaments:** three or four sixteenths, rising in velocity, always into a
  long note, at most two per solo: a chromatic climb into the high Eb, G7's b9 arpeggio into a
  cried Eb, a turn up to the soprano's G.
- **Slides (~):** a scoop into a long note (Baker's Gb→G), or a slur between two long notes
  (D sighing to C). FluidSynth only overlaps the notes; it does not glide.
- **Chords:** Tyner-style voicings: rootless 9ths and 13ths, the So What fourths shape (Bb Eb
  Ab C over F), fourths planed over the F pedal, Lydian clusters (G C D Eb for Abmaj7#11).
  Each chord is held for a bar or a half bar, then re-voiced. Grand piano behind the sax,
  Upright piano and Rhodes behind the trumpet.
- **Bass:** Garrison's two-feel in half notes, whole notes, the F pedal with its fifth, and a
  chromatic pickup into the next chord. Every note sits a little late.
- **Drums:** the brush kit only (Brush · ride; Brush · crash for the swells), darkened with
  TONE/CUTOFF. The drum chart has no per-hit velocity, so the swells are built from the
  rhythm: a crash rolled in sixteenths over the last two beats into an accented downbeat, the
  ride entering at the end of a loop. The brush swirl is sixteenth taps on the snare. In
  FluidR3's brush kit (bank 128, program 40), keys 38–40 (tap, slap, swirl) all ring for less
  than a quarter second (rendered on the laptop), so a dedicated "swirl" kit would not
  sustain, and none was added.

## Sounds

No new sounds. The tenor is FluidR3's Tenor sax (0:66; peak 0.07, warmer than the SC-88's
bright one, which Devotion uses for its edge). The soprano is FluidR3's Soprano sax (0:64;
peak 0.04, quiet: raise the lead's VOL). The trumpet is Trumpet · Cool (SC-88 0:56). All were
rendered on milo with the first version.
